## Problem Statement

Florian runs two tools: Wispr Flow for speech in and Outflow for speech out. Smart read is one shot: select, hear an overview, done. He wants "a more communicative and flowy way of interacting with an AI", and asked to "copy what free flow does and combine them into one app". Outflow 2 adds FreeFlow-style dictation and uses it to turn smart read into a conversation about what was just read.

## What Makes This Cool

- One app, one key: hold it to talk, everywhere. Your words land at the cursor like Flow does.
- The same key turns into "ask a follow-up" while a conversation is open. Hear the overview of a contract, hold the key, say "what happens if I cancel early?", hear the answer. The selection is the context, so answers are about your document, not general knowledge.
- Speech in and speech out share the provider pattern, so every half has a free offline fallback.

## Landscape

FreeFlow (Zach Latta, MIT, Swift): hold Fn, talk, release; audio goes to Groq Whisper (free tier) or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint; an LLM cleans the transcript using nearby app context and custom vocabulary; the text is pasted into the focused field. It also has voice macros and an Edit Mode that transforms selected text. No vendor server. Source: feedbagel.com and opensourcealternatives.to coverage, 2026. Outflow 2 copies the dictation loop and ideas, not the code.

## Constraints

- Everything v1 constrains still holds: Swift 6, MenuBarExtra agent app, bring your own keys in the Keychain, no server, no database, Accessibility already granted.
- New runtime permission: Microphone. Asked on first dictation, not at launch.
- Third key: Groq (optional; Apple on-device works without it).
- Paste must not lose the user's clipboard: reuse v1's text-only snapshot and transient restore.

## Premises (agreed D4)

1. Outflow absorbs FreeFlow-style dictation: hold a key, talk, release, cleaned text is pasted at the cursor. It replaces Wispr Flow.
2. Speech-to-text sits behind a Transcriber protocol: Groq Whisper default, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, Apple on-device fallback.
3. Dictation ships first as its own milestone.
4. Conversation level one is about the selection: smart read gives the overview, then holding the dictation key while a conversation is open asks a follow-up instead of pasting. Answers are spoken and kept in context.
5. A small floating panel shows the transcript, only during a conversation. Escape or a timeout ends it.
6. Reply drafting is out of scope, logged as a low-priority TODO.

Ruled out: ElevenLabs Scribe only (paid, no offline); Apple only (weak on names and German-English mix); porting FreeFlow's code (mixed architecture); a separate dictation target (packaging for a split nobody needs).

## Approaches Considered

- A) Native in OutflowCore, FreeFlow as reference. Chosen (D6).
- B) Port FreeFlow's dictation code. Rejected: two architectures in one app, less testable.
- C) Separate dictation module and target. Rejected: extra packaging, conversation needs both halves anyway.

## Recommended Approach

### New ports (OutflowCore/Ports.swift)

| Port | Live (app) | Fake (tests) |
|---|---|---|
| AudioRecorder | AVAudioEngine input tap, 16 kHz mono, writes a WAV in memory | scripted audio buffer, can fail with "no mic permission" |
| Transcriber | GroqTranscriber (OpenAI-compatible /audio/transcriptions), AppleTranscriber (SpeechAnalyzer on device) | scripted text or failure |
| TextInserter | paste at cursor: snapshot clipboard text, write transcript, press Edit > Paste via Accessibility, restore after 300 ms | records inserts |
| PushToTalkKey | CGEventTap on the chosen key (default Fn, configurable to any KeyboardShortcuts key) reporting down and up | manual down/up |

Cleanup of the raw transcript reuses AnthropicClient with a third prompt, "dictation cleanup": fix punctuation, casing and obvious mishearings, keep the speaker's words, use a custom vocabulary list from Settings. Optional: when no Anthropic key exists, the raw transcript is pasted.

### Dictation flow (milestone 1)

key down -> check mic permission -> start recording, icon shows listening -> key up -> stop -> transcribe (Groq, falls to Apple on no key, offline or error) -> optional cleanup -> TextInserter pastes at the cursor of the frozen frontmost app -> idle.
- Recordings under 0.3 s are discarded (accidental taps).
- Hard cap 2 minutes per recording; past it the recording stops and is transcribed.
- Pressing the read hotkey while dictating cancels dictation.
- Errors show in the menu exactly like v1 (Last error with actions).

### Conversation flow (milestone 2)

Smart read finishes -> a conversation opens, holding: the original selection, the overview, the frozen preset, and an empty turn list. The floating panel appears with the overview.
- While the conversation is open, the push-to-talk key means "ask": the transcript is not pasted, it becomes a user turn.
- The turn goes to Claude with the guarded system prompt, the selection still inside the document delimiters, and the prior turns as messages. The answer is spoken with the frozen preset's voice and appended to the panel.
- Pressing the key while an answer is playing stops the playback and starts listening (barge-in).
- Escape, closing the panel, or 2 minutes of silence ends the conversation. The next read or smart read starts a fresh one.
- Answer length follows the ELI10 rule: two to four short sentences unless the user asks for more.
- Context cap: the last 10 turns plus the selection; older turns are dropped first.

### Settings additions

- Dictation tab: push-to-talk key, transcriber provider (Groq / custom OpenAI-compatible URL / Apple), cleanup on or off, custom vocabulary.
- Keys tab: Groq key, with the same missing-key callout pattern as v1.
- Conversation: on or off, inactivity timeout.

## Open Questions

- Fn as push-to-talk: macOS binds Fn to the emoji picker or system dictation by default. Probe whether a CGEventTap can consume Fn reliably on macOS 26, and fall back to a configurable key (default Right Option) if not. Resolved by a day-one spike, like T1.
- Apple SpeechAnalyzer quality on mixed German and English: measure on ten real dictations against Groq.
- Paste reliability in Electron apps and terminals: same capture-matrix style check as v1.

## Success Criteria

- Key up to text at cursor at or under 1.5 s for a 10-second dictation with Groq.
- Dictation works in Safari, Mail, Notes, VS Code, Slack and Terminal; clipboard text is restored afterwards.
- A follow-up question about a smart-read contract is answered correctly from the selection, spoken within 3 s of key up.
- Barge-in stops playback within 100 ms.
- Florian uninstalls Wispr Flow after a week.

## Distribution Plan
