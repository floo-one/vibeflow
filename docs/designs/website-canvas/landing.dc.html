Pricing

Free. Works without any keys. Better with them.

VibeFlow costs nothing and has no subscription. Out of the box it reads with an Apple voice and dictates on device (macOS 26). Add a key to unlock more; you pay the provider directly, and most have a free tier. Keys live in your Keychain.

Privacy

Exactly what leaves your Mac.

There is no VibeFlow server. Text goes straight from your Mac to the provider you chose, only when you press a key. The code is public, so you can check.

Data sent per action
When youWhat is sent, and to whom
ReadThe selected text, to ElevenLabs. With an Apple voice: nothing.
Smart readThe selected text, to Anthropic, then the brief to ElevenLabs.
DictateYour recording, to Groq. Without Groq on macOS 26: nothing, it stays on device.
MeetingsNothing. Your calendars are read from Google, read-only, and kept in memory.
NeverHistory, recordings and keys. They stay on your Mac.

Questions

Do I need API keys?

No. Without any key VibeFlow reads with an Apple voice, dictates on device on macOS 26, and the clocks work. Keys unlock the ElevenLabs voice, smart read and faster dictation, and you pay those providers directly.

Do the shortcuts work on my keyboard layout?

Yes. VibeFlow uses the position, not the character: the three keys right above Option, next to M. On a German keyboard that is comma, period and minus; on a US keyboard comma, period and slash.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 15 Sequoia or later. [CONFIRM: Apple silicon and Intel?]

Why does it need Accessibility access?

To read the text you selected in other apps and to paste dictation where you were typing. It acts only when you press one of its keys.

How do updates work?

VibeFlow checks every few hours and installs a new version while you're away from the keyboard. Check for Updates… in the menu looks right away.

Why isn't it in the Mac App Store?

Store apps run in a sandbox that blocks global shortcuts and reading other apps' selections. VibeFlow is signed and notarized by Apple instead, and MIT licensed.
